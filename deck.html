The problem

Leads show up late.
Signals show up early.

Months before

The trail starts

A tender goes out. A job post goes up. A team buys new hardware. Someone talks about it on stage.

Weeks before

Nobody connects it

The clues sit in news sites, tender portals, job boards and LinkedIn. Nobody puts them together.

The day of

A form gets filled

By then the buyer has a shortlist. Maybe you are on it, maybe a competitor got there first.

For FlytBase, anyone who just bought drone docks needs software to run them. That purchase is the signal.

Your problem statement

Every buyer leaves a trail.
Build the thing that follows it.

Pick any business: yours, the one you work for, or one you love. Find the signals that its buyers are ready, and turn one into a lead with a name, a reason and a next step.

Cheat sheet

What counts as a signal?

Tenders

A refinery puts drone inspections out to bid

Hiring

A utility posts for a drone programme manager

Purchases

Someone buys the hardware that needs your product

Expansion

New sites, new cities, a fresh funding round

Regulation

A new approval or rule that unlocks the purchase

Competitors

A rival wins a deal, raises prices or gets bad reviews

Events

A talk about the exact problem you solve

Social

A post that says "this is breaking our team"

A good signal is early, points to a real organisation and makes the next step obvious.

Same problem, three angles. Every track can start from v0.1 or from scratch.

Track 01 · Design

See the signal

The brief

A salesperson has five seconds. Design how signals show up so the hottest one jumps out and the next step is obvious. Desktop, mobile, Slack card, a morning email: your call.

Finish line in 2 hours

A clickable prototype or a working screen, shown with real or realistic signals.

Ideas to steal

  • A timeline of one buyer's trail, from first clue to "call now"
  • A map of where buyers are heating up
  • Make a weak clue look weak and a hot one look urgent
  • A one-tap "write to them" flow on mobile

Handy tools

Lovable, v0, Bolt, Figma, Claude, or edit v0.1 directly

Track 02 · Strategy

Find the signal nobody is watching

The brief

Pick a business and build its signal playbook. Which moments mean a buyer is ready? Where do they show up first? How do you tell a real signal from noise?

Finish line in 2 hours

A one-page playbook with at least 3 signal sources, a simple way to score them, and one real signal found live.

Ideas to steal

  • Hit Steal the recipe in v0.1 and make it sharper
  • Find a source the crew does not know: permits, tender portals, GitHub, Reddit, app reviews
  • Decide if a tender beats a job post, and prove why
  • Map the trail backwards from a real deal you know

Handy tools

Claude, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google, a spreadsheet

Track 03 · Automation

Signal to inbox

The brief

A tool you have to remember to open is a tool nobody uses. Make signals find the team: watch a source, judge it with AI, and push the result where people already work.

Finish line in 2 hours

One flow that runs end to end on its own: source in, AI judgment, message out.

Ideas to steal

  • Run a recipe every morning and post new buyers to Slack
  • Push clues and letters into a Google Sheet or a CRM
  • Turn Quill's emails into Gmail drafts, ready to send
  • Alert only when a buyer shows two signals at once

Handy tools

n8n, Make, Zapier, Apps Script, Claude Code, Cursor

How we judge

Would someone act on this today?

Every demo answers that question, plus one test for its own track.

DESIGN

Can you spot the hottest buyer in 5 seconds?

The 5-second test: a judge sees your screen for 5 seconds, then answers "who would you contact first, and why?"

STRATEGY

Is your signal early and real?

Show the proof: one real example with its source and date, and why it comes before the buyer starts looking.

AUTOMATION

Does it run without you?

Run it live: trigger it, or show a past run, and the result lands in Slack, a sheet or an inbox with no hands on it.

3 minutes per demo. Show it working, not slides about it.
